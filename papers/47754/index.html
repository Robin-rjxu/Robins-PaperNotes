Ren et al., "A Type-II Reference-Sampling PLL with Non-Uniform Octuple-Sampling Phase Detector Achieving 55-fs JitterRMS, –91.9- dBc Reference Spur and –259-dB Jitter-Power FOM," 2024 IEEE European Solid-State Electronics Research Conference (ESSERC), Bruges, Belgium, 2024, pp. 113-116, doi: 10.1109/ESSERC62670.2024.10719490.

    IEEE Link

    Keypoints

    • dual-edge non-uniform octuple-sampling PD (OSPD)
      • time-interleaved octuple-sampling
        • not increase the REF input load
        • constant input capacitance
      • 8 sampling PDs
        • 2 differential double sampling 4 edges
        • sample each of the two edges of the REF
      • through the delay chain driven by the VCO
      • maximum increase in K_PD by 8 times
        • greater BW
        • better PN suppression of the G_M
        • 8 dB PN reduction in ~100 KHz
    • proposed RSPLL
      • scheme
        • directly samples the sinewave input REF
        • no buffer in the reference path
        • no feedback retimer
      • circuits
        • dual-edge non-uniform octuple-sampling PD (OSPD)
          • mitigate spurs
            • T-shape switches
            • multiplexed dummy sampler
            • tgate switch with complementary clocks
        • multi-input GM
          • PD’s output voltages are combined
        • loop filter
        • LC VCO
          • class-B topology
          • explicit tail tank resonating
        • multi-modulus divider (MMD)
          • internal modulus control output signal M0
          • use M0 of the 1st-stage DIV2/3
          • narrow-pulse sampling clock stream
            • cleaned by a latch inside the 1st-stage DIV23
            • eliminate the additional power-hungry retimer
          • smaller delay and lower phase noise
        • sampling clock generator
          • 2 sets of 4 phases
          • by a chain of flip-flops clocked by the VCO output
          • generated by single-to-differential converters (S2Ds)
          • locked at the mid of rising and falling edges of REF

    Background

    • High-data-rate wired and wireless communication systems
      • stringent jitter requirements (sub-100 fs)
      • trade-off : jitter, power, spurs
    • PLL architectures
      • sub-sampling PLLs
        • ultra-low in-band PN
        • decent jitter-power FoM
        • inherent high PD gain
        • requirements for low-noise REF
          • high power
          • large input capacitance load
        • narrow lock-in range : need additional FLL
      • reference sampling PLLs
        • directly sample the sinewave input
        • remove the power-hungry REF buffer
        • better locking robustness
        • good isolation to VCO
        • 3dB PN amplification
          • low slope sampling point
        • passive gain-boosting PD
          • parallel sampling
          • increased input load
    • feedback divider
      • noise will accumulate at the last stage
      • insert a flip-flop to retime the output edge
      • high-power

    Conclusion
